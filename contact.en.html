DonationWhere does my donation go?

You may have wondered, "How does WeAct care for the animals it rescues?" Today we'd like to share how the dogs we have worked hard to rescue heal from their wounds at WeAct.

Most rescued animals go straight to the hospital right after rescue. In a large-scale rescue, treatment begins in order of urgency. Considering the pain they must have suffered on the street or in a cage, they receive a range of tests, treatments and surgeries. This treatment continues until they meet their families. Some dogs were only discharged from the hospital a year after their rescue and are still in rehabilitation. Many others have chronic illnesses and have been in WeAct's care for a long time.

Once their health is stable, the dogs move to 'The Home Adoption Center.' There they regain their emotional stability and begin care and socialization training suited to each dog's temperament. We do our best to help the rescued animals recover fully, both physically and emotionally. Besides spay/neuter surgery and vaccinations, we carefully provide internal and external deworming and individual supplements such as probiotics and nutritional supplements, and we also serve freshly cooked homemade meals for extra nutrition.

We also put together a positive-reinforcement training program that reflects each dog's character to help them prepare to meet their families, and we record and closely observe every dog's temperament and health every day.

All of this is possible because of your monthly giving. WeAct works hard day and night, every day, to protect the lives it has rescued. We can say this with confidence! 🙂

Monthly giving is more than financial support. It gives the dogs a warm home, the chance to be treated right away when they are sick, and above all, a 'guardian who protects them.'

Become a guardian of these lovable lives with WeAct. Change these dogs' tomorrow with your monthly giving.

DonationWhere are donations used?

WeAct's donations are used for everything that gives rescue dogs a new life: rescuing and protecting animals in danger, treatment and rehabilitation, and training. WeAct runs on volunteering, donated skills and a minimum of paid staff, and spending on personnel costs is about 15% of total income. As a result, the share of donations used directly for the rescue dogs' food, shelter, treatment and rehabilitation is far higher than other spending.

WeAct is a designated donation organization approved by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and fulfills its duties as a public-interest corporation on the basis of trustworthiness and transparency through an independent external audit every year. We disclose how donations are managed through a monthly financial report, which you can check on the WeAct website at any time.

AdoptionI don't know much about pets. How should I prepare for adoption?

It's okay if you don't know much about pets. Adoption is less about being perfectly prepared than about taking responsibility as a guardian, learning what you lack, and filling in each other's gaps. Still, because these are rescued animals, some basic understanding and preparation before adoption are necessary. During the adoption process, WeAct explains the daily life and responsibilities of living with a pet through consultations and interviews, and we check together your circumstances and how prepared you are.

What matters is the consent of every family member and an environment and mindset that let you take responsibility to the end. Even if you lack experience, if you are willing to learn, you can prepare for adoption step by step with WeAct.

You can request an adoption consultation through the link below.

AdoptionWhy is WeAct's adoption process different?

The road to adoption may seem long and hard, but WeAct's thorough adoption process is the minimum safeguard for rescue dogs. The rescued dogs have gone through more hardship and pain than we can imagine. Dogs who have lived hard lives find great comfort and happiness with a family. Please think of this as the first step of the warm heart and helping hand that considers adopting a rescue dog, and give your whole heart to every part of the process for the dog you hope to adopt.

RescueI would like to request a rescue or file a report with WeAct. What should I do?

Please click the 'Report' button in the 'Contact' menu and submit a report about an animal that needs rescue.

Enter the information about the abuse you are reporting, such as details about the abused animals and the address of the site, along with your own personal information such as your name, phone number and email. After you agree to the use of your personal information and click the submit button, your report is complete. Based on what you wrote, the rescue team discusses it internally and decides whether to rescue, and we do not contact you separately about whether a rescue goes ahead.

The site information needed to request a rescue is as follows.

1. The exact address of the site you are reporting - Please find the exact address of the site where a rescue is needed. A map app can help you find it. If it is vague or inaccurate, we may not be able to find the site of the abuse, or the rescue may be delayed.

2. The number of animals at the site - The number of animals needing rescue is an indicator of the scale of the rescue. Even if it is not exact, a rough estimate helps.

3. A description of the site - Please describe in detail what you witnessed: the animals' health as visible to the naked eye, signs of abuse, the surrounding environment and so on. Details play a very important role in preparing for a rescue.

4. Whether there are other animals besides dogs at the site - Please be sure to tell us if there are animals other than dogs, so we can prepare rescue scenarios for every situation.

5. Photo or video evidence related to the report - Please share the site with photos and videos. Plenty of photos and videos help us visualize the site and can serve as very important evidence of abuse.

6. How you could help with the rescue - A rescue is a beginning, not an end. After a rescue, the animals heal their hurt bodies and hearts and go through preparation before they meet a family. Please tell us how you could cooperate in the rescue. Continued rescues are possible only because of the help and effort of so many people. Feel free to write down anything you can do to help the animals in need, and we will take it into account.

Report to WeAct

RescueI want to file a complaint or report about a dog meat farm or puppy mill. What should I do?

Evidence needs to be collected about the site of the dog meat farm or puppy mill. For dog meat farms and puppy mills, rather than filing a complaint about animal abuse alone, you should file complaints about all the illegal conditions at the site. That way, you address not only the immediate abuse but also the problems with the site itself so that it cannot continue, and the site is made to disappear. For this, the more evidence the better.

[Complaint guide: please refer to this section if you want to file a complaint]

1. Check the land use: Look up the land register for the address and check the land use plan and restrictions on activities.

Restrictions on activities, such as the limit on the number of livestock, differ by jurisdiction. To find out, you need to check the local ordinance of the relevant area, and the laws that can be applied also differ by region depending on the land's use, so this needs to be checked separately. See also: the Farmland Act, Agricultural Promotion Zones, the Mountainous Districts Management Act, the Road Act, etc.

Go to land register search

2. Check whether the structures are illegal

You can file a complaint about structures that have not been reported or that are illegal. However, they cannot be demolished immediately after a report. The process proceeds through steps such as corrective order -> demand -> notice of enforcement fine -> imposition of enforcement fine, and these steps do not continue unless complaints are filed continuously along the way. So if you want the site demolished, you must keep filing complaints.

(Enforcement fines can be imposed twice a year)

See also: Building Act Articles 14 and 20

Go to building register issuance

3. If dogs are being slaughtered

Under Article 10, Paragraph 1 of the Animal Protection Act, slaughter counts as animal abuse. You can file a complaint under Article 10, Paragraph 1.

See also: Animal Protection Act Article 10 (Prohibition of Animal Abuse), Paragraph 1, each subparagraph

4. Management and use of livestock manure

You can file a complaint under Article 17 of the Act on the Management and Use of Livestock Excreta, and you can request administrative action under Articles 18 and 50.

5. Food waste management

You can file a complaint under Article 25 of the Wastes Control Act, Article 14 of the Control of Livestock and Fish Feed Act, and Article 11 of the Standards and Specifications for Feed.

Other laws worth consulting together:

Appendix 19 'Substances prohibited from use as feed'

Appendix 9 'Standards for sterilization and pasteurization of feed'

Animal Protection Act Article 9, each paragraph

6. Waste management

You can file a complaint under Articles 13 and 66 of the Wastes Control Act.

7. Sewage treatment

Sewerage Act

You can file a complaint under Article 34.

8. Restrictions on discharge facilities

You can file a complaint under Article 15 of the Water Environment Conservation Act, and you can request administrative action under Article 78.

It would be best to collect evidence for all of the above, but even partial evidence is enough to report the site and file a complaint.

If you want to file a complaint with the evidence you have collected, you can do so as follows.

1. Go to the e-People (Kookmin Sinmungo) website (www.epeople.go.kr).

2. Click to file a complaint, then log in as a member or non-member.

3. Fill out the application.

3-1. Check the required consent boxes, enter your personal information, and click Next.

3-2. Select a report-type complaint and enter the information of the person being reported.

3-3. Write the evidence and details you collected in the complaint title and content, and add attachments.

4. Select the agency to handle it. Choose the animal-related department of the local government where the dog meat farm or puppy mill is located.

VolunteeringAre all of WeAct's activists volunteers?

Apart from the activists who care for and train the rescue dogs at WeAct's THE HOME and the small number who handle the finance and organizational management a non-profit incorporated association needs, 80% of the activists who make up WeAct have regular jobs and take part through donated skills and volunteering, without activity fees.

WeAct activists are divided into crew and actors according to their duties and level of activity, and are recruited from time to time through announcements on WeAct's Instagram.

VolunteeringHow can I become a WeAct crew member or actor?

WeAct crew members and actors are recruited from time to time through Instagram announcements when we need more people. After applying, selection is decided through a document review and a video interview, and crew members first go through a three-month period as an actor.

Even outside the recruitment period for crew and actors, if you contact WeAct by email about donating your skills, we will review it internally and consider where we can work together, so we welcome your interest.

Everyone who makes up WeAct today has a different day job, and we differ in age, where we live and how we live, but we have come together with one shared goal, to give suffering dogs a new life, and we lead WeAct through volunteering and donated skills.

Ordinary people, from where they are, drawing on their own experience and adding their own abilities and expertise, are working to improve, even a little, the world that animals in Korea live in. We are waiting for new faces of WeAct who want to work for animals and who have warm but strong hearts.

VolunteeringWhich regions are available for overseas flight volunteering, and how do I apply?

Overseas flight volunteering is available to San Francisco, Las Vegas and New York in the United States, and Toronto in Canada. Other areas in the Americas are accepted when needed, and Europe, Australia and Asia are not accepted.

Airline information

● For the US, only Korean Air and Asiana are possible, and for Canada, Air Canada is also possible.

● Codeshare flights are not possible. (For example, a Delta-Korean Air codeshare.)

● Air Premia is not accepted.

VolunteeringWhat is the process for overseas flight volunteering?

Before departure:

1. Based on what you wrote in the application, we reserve a spot for the dog with the airline. Rescue dogs usually travel as checked baggage, so one person can reserve two checked baggage spots. After reserving, we contact you by KakaoTalk.

2. About two weeks before departure, we introduce the rescue dog confirmed to travel and briefly explain the flight volunteering procedure.

On the day of departure:

1. On the day of departure, please meet a WeAct activist at the airport check-in counter. Please arrive 3 hours before departure. Please note that mobile check-in and self check-in are not possible when traveling with a dog.

2. Before check-in, we explain the documents needed for flight volunteering, and there is a step where we take photos with the rescue dogs.

3. After check-in, the WeAct activist pays the excess baggage fee, and you can then head to the departure area.

On arrival at the local airport (USA):

1. Immigration: Immigration officers usually do not ask about the rescue dogs. If you are asked, show the documents we gave you at the airport and say "Flight Volunteer."

2. Collect your own luggage: Please collect your own luggage first.

3. Pick up the rescue dogs: The dogs are usually brought out separately at the oversized baggage area. If you cannot see the dogs, please ask an airline staff member. Be sure to use a porter service or a paid cart; the porter fee is paid by the pickup volunteer once you have fully exited and met them.

4. Customs: Customs declaration forms have recently been dropped. Show the documents we gave you to the customs officer, and when customs is finished, make sure you get the documents back. In the US, all customs procedures for the rescue dogs are completed before departure, so only a document check is needed. (It takes about 10 minutes.)

5. Exit through the arrivals gate and meet the local pickup volunteer. Hand over the rescue dogs together with the documents.

On arrival at the local airport (Canada):

1. Immigration: Immigration officers usually do not ask about the rescue dogs. If you are asked, show the documents we gave you at the airport and say "Flight Volunteer."

2. Collect your own luggage: Please collect your own luggage first.

3. Pick up the rescue dogs: The dogs are usually brought out separately at the oversized baggage area. If you cannot see the dogs, please ask an airline staff member. Please be sure to use a porter service or a paid cart. We pay the porter fee.

4. Customs: Customs is handled after arrival. Take the rescue dogs to Agriculture and hand the documents to the officer. (It takes about 30 minutes.)

5. Exit through the arrivals gate with the rescue dogs and you will meet the pickup volunteer outside the airport.

VolunteeringCan minors volunteer?

For safety reasons, WeAct allows only healthy adult volunteers aged 20 or older. Minors are not allowed to volunteer even when accompanied by a guardian. We hope to see you again when you are an adult and have decided to join us for the animals who have been left behind.
