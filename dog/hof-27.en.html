Meet Guri

Meet Guri, with charming short legs, a long back and a raccoon-like look! Calm and gentle, yet curious about the world, Guri (❁´◡`❁).

At first Guri looks around carefully, but before long approaches and shows its curiosity everywhere! Guri is actually a charmer. Guri comes over shyly and waits to be petted, as if saying “Adore me!” When Guri sees someone it has grown close to, it jumps with joy and trots after them. Guri especially likes a gentle stroke on the top of the head and treats.

Before you bring them home

Health

Guri has chronic kidney failure and needs regular check-ups and health care. Guri also has a history of left hind leg fracture surgery, so activity needs to be adjusted while watching recovery. During a rest outside, Guri once coughed severely for about 1 to 2 minutes, so this also needs to be watched.

Needs help with

Guri knows how to use indoor pee pads, but depending on the environment may have accidents or mark. Please keep guiding Guri so it can get used to the toilet spot and routines in the new home. Because Guri is recovering from a fracture, full walk training has not been done yet, and Guri needs time to learn slowly according to its physical condition.

Ideal family

We hope for a family who understands Guri’s health condition and will keep up regular check-ups and daily care. We hope for someone who will adjust activity to the recovery pace and give Guri plenty of time to adapt to a new environment! We are waiting for a family who will offer a kind hand to shy Guri, who likes both people and friends, and share small changes and joys together.

The day Guri was rescuedRescued at · Jeonnam dog meat farm

More than two years have passed since the law banning dog meat was promulgated. The government set a 3-year grace period before penalties to let people in the industry prepare to change or close their businesses, and this summer is the last summer of that grace period. Because it is the last summer for “boshin” (eating dog meat for stamina), the last time to make money by killing dogs… countless dogs were again ending their lives in elevated wire cages. A dog farm in Gangjin, Jeollanam-do. The dogs from when the tip was received were gone, and the farm had already been filled with new dogs. The dogs who became stars at the hands of a farm owner trying to squeeze out every last won must have wagged their tails even at the moment they were taken away. To someone, they may have been just a bowl of dog meat soup, but they were kind, pure dogs who smiled at people even with terrified faces. To end this cruel cycle, WeAct decided to rescue, and nine dogs who had been locked in elevated wire cages where their feet sank through finally escaped the hell-like iron cages. Nine lives that almost became “food” for someone’s stamina are now preparing to begin their second chapter of life as proud “companion dogs.” We will not stop until the day this contradictory dog meat culture, in which dogs are seen as family companions by some and as food by others, ends completely. For the nine lives who trusted people to the very end, we will find a safe and clean home and the warm embrace of a family who will share love.

Day to day

With people
