Meet Vita

Vita pours out love from the moment you meet, and likes everyone, regardless of age or gender. She also gets along well with dog friends, so she seems to enjoy interacting and building relationships. But she is also independent and has a mature side: she needs some quiet time alone each day. She is not sensitive to touch or grooming, and she is a well-behaved dog who rides well in a car seat. She has many charms, including jumping up beside you when you prepare her meal and following you around trying to snatch the treats you are holding, showing her greedy side.

Before you bring them home

Health

Right after the rescue, she had idiopathic seizures. She is currently managed with prescription medication and has not had seizures, but she gets startled by certain noises in daily life. She currently takes medication twice a day, morning and evening, and needs regular checkups.

Needs help with

She knows how to walk on a leash, but she sometimes tries to eat fallen leaves on the ground and dislikes tension on the leash. She needs steady training for safer and more enjoyable walks. If you put down a potty pad in a set spot, she uses it 80 to 90 percent of the time. However, when adjusting to a new space, she may mark at first if there is a resident dog. She shows wariness by barking when she is touched while resting or when another dog passes close by, so appropriate responses are needed.

Ideal family

A stable, calm environment suits Vita. Rather than a home with lots of travel or outside activity, we hope she can spend relaxed time in a cozy space, sharing gentle bonding. Rather than a noisy home with young children, we hope for a family of adults who can share their hearts with her through moderate physical contact and subtle affection. It is also better if Vita is not left alone for long periods. From what we saw in foster care, she doesn't rely on other dog friends and is often bothered by them, so she is better as an only dog. Vita needs to keep going to the hospital and taking medication. So she needs a guardian who understands managing a dog's illness and can devote time and effort. We are waiting for a family ready to give the warmest place in their lives to fluffy Vita, whose whole body is full of love.

The day Vita was rescuedRescued at · Licensed puppy mill in Seongju

In March 2024, a plea from a puppy mill owner, begging us to save the dogs, reached WeAct. This breeder started in 2017 with 20 Bichon Frises and had tried in their own way to operate within the law. But they could not withstand the pressure of the market, with auctioneers and pet shop owners who wanted only ever smaller animals. The even smaller breeding dogs they bought at high prices to get ever smaller puppies were, unsurprisingly, often sick, and their puppies could hardly be healthy either. Treating sick animals pushed up operating costs, and debts quickly piled up. The breeder could not bring themselves to send the so-called "discarded dogs", those thrown away once they are no longer useful, to places that slaughter them for meat or to even worse puppy mills, and finally appealed to fellow organizations, including WeAct, to rescue the dogs. In total, 290 dogs were rescued and shared among the organizations. WeAct took in 21 of them. The friends rescued with her soon found families that suited them perfectly, but Vita, even though she is a so-called purebred, is still waiting endlessly for a forever family, perhaps because of her health issues.
