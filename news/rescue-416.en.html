A night of dog-tired work

A dog of a night

Every rescue site unfolds unpredictably, but this one in Hwacheon was truly hard to believe. WeAct left at 9 a.m. and could not leave the site until after 3 a.m. the next day.

At midday, with the heat still lingering, in a remote forest with barely any phone signal, the activists crossed the mountain and searched the site for about 3 hours to find the other dogs of the person who claims to be Huimang's owner.

Pushing through sharp grass taller than we were, and opening every box and pot we could see one by one, we finally found dogs and rabbits.

Meeting the abuser was not easy either. Even the neighbors waved us off, saying he was "someone you gain nothing from talking to." WeAct waited three and a half hours to meet him. After a long wait and conversation, he said the animals he was keeping were 5 dogs and 2 rabbits.

He also told us that he had long run a dog meat farm and a puppy mill, and that #WeActHuimang was a precious dog he had kept for over 10 years, a dog who "had many puppies and earned a lot of money." He said this precious dog's name was 'Baekgu.'

The abuser insisted he never mistreated animals. But the dogs we saw with our own eyes were very different from his account. One dog, apparently starving, was tearing at the carcass of a chicken, and its food bowl was crawling with insects we could barely make out.

The dogs wore heavily rusted collars, connected to thick iron chains about 1 m long fixed firmly to the ground and trees. Collars they could never escape unless they died.

After a long conversation with the abuser, WeAct finally obtained the surrender of ownership at 11 p.m. But the real problem came after that.

In case he interfered with the rescue, one activist stayed by the abuser's side for over 4 hours, keeping him company and holding him in place. The other 4 activists continued the rescue in pitch darkness on a mountain without a single streetlight, relying on the small light of their phones. Ignoring the ticks and insects clinging to us from every side, we pushed through rough grass and went back and forth over a long distance many times.

We carefully rescued the frightened, extremely wary dogs one by one, and carried heavy kennels out to the unpaved road, gasping for breath.

Persuading an owner for hours, who firmly refused to give up ownership to the end, claiming he raised them this way because he loved animals, patiently and carefully rescuing the especially wary dogs safely, and carrying heavy kennels out of the deep mountains to the very end were all possible because you were watching with us.

Thanks to you, the activists at the site did not feel alone and could keep up the rescue without tiring. Thanks also to Boram, who brought kennels all the way from Chuncheon even after midnight to make up for our shortage, we were able to bring the dogs to safety.

The rescued dogs are wary and frightened, so it is difficult to take them for veterinary care right away. We plan to let them rest in a safe place for now and then check their health closely.

We will see this through to the end, so that these dogs never go back to that forest where no footsteps or traces of life reach, and can start a new life in a safe place.

P.S. As the saying goes, it takes a village to raise a child. Thanks to WeAct's donors and subscribers, to Boram, who gladly came a long way at an absurd hour at a sudden request for transport, to the owner of Urigo Pet Taxi and his wife, and to the family at Dringen Training Center, who gladly took in the dogs even at dawn, we were able to save Huimang's friends.

Thank you so much for ACTing with us.

The complaint against the abuser for animal cruelty and other violations is scheduled to be filed after August 21.
We will share that news as well.
